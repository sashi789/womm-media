Anyone on this Wi-Fi can read plain pages. | How does the HTTPS padlock stop them?

Plain HTTP is a postcard: readable by anyone who handles it. HTTPS is a sealed envelope.

The handshake before any page loads:
1. Your browser says hello, lists what it supports, and sends half of a secret
2. The site replies with its half, plus its certificate
3. Your browser checks the certificate: signed by a trusted authority, name matches the site
4. Both sides combine the halves into the same key, and every message is encrypted with it

The trick is a key exchange: like each side adding a secret colour to a public colour and swapping the mixes. A spy sees the mixes, never the secrets. (Real TLS uses numbers, not paint.)

The padlock means encrypted and name-matched. It does not mean the site is honest.
