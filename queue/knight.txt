45 minutes. One forgotten server. | How a deployment mistake cost $460 million

On August 1, 2012, Knight Capital lost more than $460 million in about 45 minutes. Not a hack. A deployment.

What the SEC found:
1. New code was copied to 7 of the 8 servers that route orders. One technician missed the eighth, and nobody reviewed the deployment
2. The new feature reused an old on/off flag that used to control a retired feature called Power Peg. That old code was still on the servers
3. On the eighth server, the flag woke the old code. Its stop counter had been moved years earlier and never retested, so it never stopped
4. 212 orders became millions of trades: over 4 million executions in 154 stocks
5. 97 automatic emails saying "Power Peg disabled" arrived before the market opened. They were not designed as alerts and weren't acted on
6. Rolling back the new code from the 7 good servers made it worse

Lessons: a second person reviews every deploy, delete dead code instead of reusing its flag, and alerts must reach a human who acts.

Would your team have caught it? Tell me in the comments.

Source: SEC order, In the Matter of Knight Capital Americas LLC, Release No. 34-70694 (October 16, 2013). Knight paid a $12 million penalty.
