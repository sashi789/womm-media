Confident. Fluent. Wrong. | Why AI makes things up

In 2023, two New York lawyers filed a court brief with six cases found by ChatGPT. None of the cases existed, and the judge fined them $5,000.

Why it happens:
1. A chatbot doesn't look facts up. It writes the next word that is likely to fit, based on patterns in its training text
2. A made-up case name looks exactly like a real one, because the model knows what those look like
3. Rare facts (a birthday seen once) have no pattern to learn, so it fills the gap
4. Models are graded like an exam where a blank answer scores zero, so guessing beats saying "I don't know"

What helps: give it the source text, check every name, number, quote and citation, and ask it to say when it isn't sure. That reduces the risk but doesn't remove it.
